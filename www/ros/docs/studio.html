Reload OS Studio

A screen designer in the browser, in the manner of Android Studio's layout editor. The screen in the middle is the real interface: the same LVGL, shell, fonts and theme as on a device, compiled to WebAssembly. What you draw exports as C for the app kit. Know Android? Coming from Android says what each thing is called here.

Open Studio

How it works

  1. Pick a board in the toolbar, and turn the screen or switch to the light theme. The status bar is the real one.

  2. Drag widgets from the Palette onto the screen or click to add one. A Column or a Row lays out what is in it; a Free area keeps each widget where you put it, anchored to a corner, an edge or the centre. Tabs has a page a tab: click a tab on the screen (or its page in the tree) to work on it, + Add a tab in Attributes for another, and say which one the app opens on. A Picture button is a picture to tap: it darkens while pressed.

  3. Set them up in Attributes. Sizes that fill or fit their content move with the screen; pixel sizes do not. Colours by name follow the light and dark theme on the device.

  4. Style them (Attributes › Style): background and its opacity, text colour, border, corners, shadow, opacity and padding - for the normal look and, on its own tab, while the widget is pressed, checked or disabled. A dot on a tab says that look is set; Clear the look takes it back to the theme's.

  5. Say what happens (Attributes › Events): a widget can have several events, each when it is tapped, held, pressed, let go or its value changes, and do one thing - show a message, show a dialog (a box with a title, a text and OK), open a screen, go back, play a sound, call a function of yours, set another widget's text or value, show or hide it, enable or disable it, check or uncheck it, or play its animation. In a text, {value} is the widget's own value: a slider's number, a switch's On / Off, a dropdown's choice, what was typed - so Brightness {value}% on a label follows a slider without a line of code. A tab view's value is its tab: Set a value on it switches the tab (the first is 0). A screen's events run each time it is shown (select the screen, then Events). A screen can come in fading or sliding (Screen › Comes in), and play a sound as it does (Screen › Plays); the way back is the way in, reversed.

  6. Animate (Attributes › Animation): a widget fades in, slides in from a side, blinks, floats or shakes - its time, a wait before it, once, a few times or for ever, when the screen opens or when an event plays it. Play shows it on the screen.

  7. The app's own colours and text size: select the app (top of Project). Colours of the app are yours by name - every colour list offers them, and changing one there changes every widget that uses it; Font size is the size of every text that does not say its own.

  8. My components: right click a widget (a card with all that is in it, say) › Save as a component. It appears in the Palette under My components; dragging it out makes a copy with ids of its own. Components are kept with the design.

  9. Edit fast: a right click on the screen or in the tree has cut, copy, paste, duplicate, delete, move up and down, wrap in a column / row / card and unwrap, hide and lock. Shift or Cmd / Ctrl and a click picks several: line them up by an edge or the middle, spread them evenly, move them together. In a Free area a widget being dragged lines up with its neighbours and the area (pink guides; Alt to place it freely). A locked widget can't be picked or moved on the screen - the tree still can. ? lists the keys.

  10. Pictures and sounds of your own: Assets › + takes picture files (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP) and sound files (WAV, MP3, OGG, M4A). A picture becomes a PNG no larger than the screen; a sound a 16 kHz mono WAV, the first 60 s. A Picture widget shows one - as it is, or scaled to its size, stretched, cut or tiled; Run plays the sounds in the browser. They go inside the app's .elf: one file to copy, nothing else on the device.

  11. Run tries it on the screen, with the mouse as a finger and Esc as the back swipe: the events and the animations run as they will on the device.

  12. Problems at the bottom checks the screen on every board, both ways round: a widget that runs off the edge, a name C can't use.

  13. Sign in (top right) to keep your designs on our server - Save (Ctrl+S) and Open, from any computer, pictures and sounds included - and to use Export › ELF. An account is an email address and a password; we send a mail to confirm the address, and one with a link when you forget the password. Without an account Studio works as it always did, in your browser.

  14. Export › ELF builds the app on our server with the app kit and gives you the .elf for the board you picked: copy it into apps/ on the device's flash drive or SD card and restart the device. A compiler error comes back into Problems. Nothing of your app stays on the server.

  15. Export › Project folder gives a zip of the app's folder instead, for the app kit on your own machine (ESP-IDF 6.1) - when you write more of app.c than Studio shows:

    unzip myapp.zip -d appkit
cd appkit
./build.sh myapp jc         # -> myapp/jc1060p470c/myapp.elf

What you get

FileWhat it is
main/ui.c, main/ui.hThe screens, made from the design. Don't edit them: change the design and export again. ui.h has the widgets by their id, ui.title; a meter's needle is ui.gauge_needle, a spinbox's row of buttons ui.qty_box.
main/app.cYours: the app's start, and the functions the widgets call. A function you have not written yet shows a message instead.
myapp.designThe design. Open it in Studio to change the screens, then copy the new ui.c and ui.h over the old ones.

Good to know

  • Signed out, your design stays in this browser (and in the .design file: Export › Design file). Signed in, Save keeps it in your account too: its password is stored as a salted scrypt hash, and nothing of your designs is shared with anyone.
  • Studio can use what an app may call on a device: the code stays inside reloados/api.txt.
  • Pictures and sounds stay in this browser too (and in the .design file). The app's file grows with them and is loaded into the device's memory whole: Problems warns above 2 MB, and the build server takes 6 MB. A picture is decoded once while the app runs and freed when it closes. In the C: ui_picture(UI_PIC_name) and ui_sound(UI_SND_name); the files are main/assets/, embedded by main/CMakeLists.txt. They work on firmware 1.4.0 too.
  • Run shows the design. It does not run your app.c: that needs a device.
  • Keypad keys: a row a line, a comma between keys; a key named like an LVGL symbol (OK, BACKSPACE, LEFT ...) shows the symbol. A calendar with no date shows the device's month.
  • The calendar, meter, spinbox, LED, keypad, toggle buttons, the screen transitions and the fade, slide and shake animations came after firmware 1.4.0: a device on 1.4.0 or older can't open an app that uses them (Settings › About › Check online). Styles, events, blink and float work on 1.4.0, and so do tabs, dialogs, picture buttons and the app's colours and text size - except a tab view that opens on another tab than its first, or whose tab an event sets. Thai text in a design needs the newer firmware too.
  • In the C: the app's colours are UI_C_NAME in ui.h; a dialog is ui_dialog(title, text), one at a time, closed with the app; a tab's page is ui.<id> like any box.
  • In the C an event is a callback, <id>_cb (<id>_long_cb for a hold ...), with its actions in order; an animation is anim_<id>(); a screen's events are the last lines of its build_<screen>(). A function of yours called from a screen's events gets NULL for the event.
  • The build server builds one app at a time; a board's first build of the day can take a few minutes. It accepts the three sources only, and an #include of a header by its name.
